## How to read a finding

Every finding carries three separate values. They answer different questions, and one never stands in for another.

- **Severity:** how bad it would be if true. `critical`, `high`, `medium`, `low`, or `info`. `info` means true but not dangerous in itself, such as inventory or posture.
- **Confidence:** how sure we are that it is true, from 0 to 1. It starts at 0.5, because we know nothing about a target before we look. The evidence then moves it. Confidence is provisional until calibration (Phase 4).
- **State:** what we are prepared to say, computed from severity, confidence and validation by `core/state.py`. A scanner never sets it by hand.

**The five states:**

| State | Means | When |
|---|---|---|
| `confirmed` | We proved it | A validation step ran **and passed**, and confidence is ≥ 0.95 |
| `highly_likely` | Two independent kinds of evidence agree | Confidence ≥ 0.75 and the signals come from **two or more independence classes** |
| `suspected` | Real evidence, one source | Confidence ≥ 0.40 with one independence class or no validation. Also any finding whose validation **failed** |
| `informational` | True, not a weakness | Every `info`-severity finding |
| `inconclusive` | We could not tell, and say why | The prerequisite failed, was blocked, or was ambiguous. Never a silent "nothing found" |

Below 0.40 confidence nothing is reported.

**Only `confirmed` and `highly_likely` count as claims.** They are what precision is measured on and what `--fail-on` breaks a build for. `suspected` is shown, but it is a hedge, not a claim. Triage sorts by severity × confidence.

**Light and Standard.**
- **Light** is passive: ordinary requests anyone could make, such as DNS lookups, normal page loads, normal TLS handshakes, and public registries and logs. It needs no proof that you own the target.
- **Standard** adds more checks. Every Standard check in these four scanners is *declared* passive under [ADR-023](02-decisions.md#adr-023--passive-is-ordinary-negotiation-only-malformed-input-is-active), so `core/authz.py` lets a Standard scan run with no ownership record (ledger D27). The ownership gate is built and applies to the first check declared *active*. The four testssl.sh checks sent active probes despite their declaration until ledger D56 was fixed on 2026-10-12, and since D59 they do not run outside the lab (see the tls section). [ADR-033](02-decisions.md#adr-033--mvp-alpha-scope-one-combined-release-the-linux-agent-moves-into-alpha-tool-4b-deferred) sets who may run Standard in the Alpha.
- Deep/Validated mode does not exist yet.

**Status column.**
- **WORKING:** fired correctly on a real, live target and was checked by hand. For an informational check, it was observed producing correct inventory live.
- **BROKEN:** known not to work in production. The ledger row is cited.
- **UNDETERMINED:** no known defect, but no live evidence that meets the standard. The check was proven only against our corpus or lab, or is under re-verification.

**Availability column** (added 2026-10-12). Whether a customer's scan runs the check at all, in its stated mode. It is a separate fact from Status, and one never stands in for the other. Status records whether the check has been shown to fire correctly on real evidence. Availability records whether a customer can reach it.
- **Available:** a customer-facing scan runs it in the mode shown.
- **Unavailable, with the reason:** no customer-facing scan runs it, whatever the target. Its Status and evidence stay as recorded, so a check verified and then withheld is never confused with one never verified. Today the only reason is **consent condition unproven** (ledger D59): the four testssl.sh checks need an address shown to be dedicated to the customer, and nothing can establish that yet.

## web — Website Scanner (Tool 4a, v1.0.7)

21 checks, all Light, all passive. At most 12 page requests per scan, though redirect hops can add more (D26).

**Header checks share one rule.** They reach `confirmed` only when the deficiency is seen on the homepage **and** on a second page of the same site: a deliberate 404 control, or for the redirect check a second plain-HTTP path. That shows the policy is site-wide. Both responses can come through the same edge, so it shows one site's configuration twice, not independent infrastructure agreeing.

**Some states always come out `inconclusive`, with the cause named.** This happens on a bot-challenge page, when the homepage redirects off-site, or when HTTPS fails, which applies to the HSTS checks.

## email — Email Security Scanner (Tool 1, v1.5.2)

**DMARC is read under RFC 9989 first** (v1.4.0). Where RFC 7489, still deployed, gives a different verdict, the finding states both and is `suspected`, at the severity of the worst case (ADR-017). **Which DMARC record applies** (v1.3.0) is found by RFC 9989's DNS tree walk (§4.10). A subdomain inherits its parent's `sp=` (or `np=`) policy. An inherited policy is stated under both RFCs and held at `suspected`, because RFC 7489 receivers choose with a Public Suffix List we do not consult (D49). Several DMARC records at one name are all discarded, as both RFCs require.

Nothing is sent to the domain's mail servers. 10 run in Light, and 10 more in Standard. *(Regenerated 2026-10-12: this line said 18 checks and 8 in Standard; the code has 20 and 10.)* `secret-exposed` is not in the scanner's `CHECKS` table: core's helper emits it (`core/exposure.py`, ADR-029).

## tls — SSL/TLS Analyzer (Tool 2, v1.4.3)

19 checks, declared passive under ADR-023 (ordinary TLS handshakes). 8 run in Light, and 11 more in Standard, of which **4 are unavailable to customers** (see the Availability column). **Ledger D56, fixed 2026-10-12 (PR #44):** the four testssl.sh checks below used to run `testssl.sh --vulnerable`, whose malformed-input probes ADR-023 classes as active. They now run only the four cipher tests they read (`--rc4 --sweet32 --freak --logjam`), under an exact argument and environment allowlist enforced at runtime ([ADR-023](02-decisions.md#adr-023--passive-is-ordinary-negotiation-only-malformed-input-is-active), D56 amendment). testssl.sh also sends connection- and protocol-detection handshakes, one ordinary `GET /` and one large well-formed ClientHello on every run, all accepted as passive there. **Ledger D59 (2026-10-12): these four do not run outside the lab.** Their SSLv2 hellos carry no SNI, so on a shared address they would reach a site that is not the customer's, and we cannot yet show an address is dedicated. Live Standard scans report them `inconclusive` with that reason. Their Status below rests on the badssl.com recordings, made under an explicit lab override, and stays as recorded. Their Availability column says they are unavailable to every customer, and why.

**No tls check has a validation step, so none reaches `confirmed`.** The ceiling is `highly_likely`. It is reached only when two independent readings agree: our own reading of the certificate, and the trust path (OpenSSL's verification). Single-reading checks stop at `suspected`.

Four Standard checks (`tls-vuln-rc4`, `-sweet32`, `-freak`, `-logjam`) are probed by **testssl.sh**, run as a separate process. Our own engine's OpenSSL cannot offer those ciphers at all.

## domscout — DNS & Subdomain Enumeration (Tool 3, v1.4.0)

7 checks, all Light, all passive (`secret-exposed` is emitted by core's helper, ADR-029). Every lookup goes to a public service (CT logs, public resolvers, RDAP), never to the target, apart from at most one ordinary HTTPS page load per takeover candidate.
