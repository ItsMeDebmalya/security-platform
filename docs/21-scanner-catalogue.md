## How to read a finding

Every finding carries three separate values. They answer different questions, and one never stands in for another.

- **Severity:** how bad it would be if true. `critical`, `high`, `medium`, `low`, or `info`. `info` means true but not dangerous in itself, such as inventory or posture.
- **Confidence:** how sure we are that it is true, from 0 to 1. It starts at 0.5, because we know nothing about a target before we look. The evidence then moves it. Confidence is provisional until calibration (Phase 4).
- **State:** what we are prepared to say, computed from severity, confidence and validation by `core/state.py`. A scanner never sets it by hand.

**The five states:**

| State | Means | When |
|---|---|---|
| `confirmed` | We proved it | A validation step ran **and passed**, and confidence is ≥ 0.95 |
| `highly_likely` | Two independent kinds of evidence agree | Confidence ≥ 0.75 and the signals come from **two or more independence classes** |
| `suspected` | Real evidence, one source | Confidence ≥ 0.40 with one independence class or no validation. Also any finding whose validation **failed** |
| `informational` | True, not a weakness | Every `info`-severity finding |
| `inconclusive` | We could not tell, and say why | The prerequisite failed, was blocked, or was ambiguous. Never a silent "nothing found" |

Below 0.40 confidence nothing is reported.

**Only `confirmed` and `highly_likely` count as claims.** They are what precision is measured on and what `--fail-on` breaks a build for. `suspected` is shown, but it is a hedge, not a claim. Triage sorts by severity × confidence.

**Light and Standard.**
- **Light** is passive: ordinary requests anyone could make, such as DNS lookups, normal page loads, normal TLS handshakes, and public registries and logs. It needs no proof that you own the target.
- **Standard** adds more checks. Today every Standard check in these four scanners is also passive under [ADR-023](02-decisions.md#adr-023--passive-is-ordinary-negotiation-only-malformed-input-is-active), so a Standard scan reaches no ownership gate (ledger D27). The ownership gate is built and applies to the first *active* check, whenever one ships.
- Deep/Validated mode does not exist yet.

**Status column.**
- **WORKING:** fired correctly on a real, live target and was checked by hand. For an informational check, it was observed producing correct inventory live.
- **BROKEN:** known not to work in production. The ledger row is cited.
- **UNDETERMINED:** no known defect, but no live evidence that meets the standard. The check was proven only against our corpus or lab, or is under re-verification.

## web — Website Scanner (Tool 4a, v1.0.3)

21 checks, all Light, all passive. At most 12 page requests per scan, though redirect hops can add more (D26).

**Header checks share one rule.** They reach `confirmed` only when the deficiency is seen on the homepage **and** on a second page of the same site: a deliberate 404 control, or for the redirect check a second plain-HTTP path. That shows the policy is site-wide. Both responses can come through the same edge, so it shows one site's configuration twice, not independent infrastructure agreeing.

**Some states always come out `inconclusive`, with the cause named.** This happens on a bot-challenge page, when the homepage redirects off-site, or when HTTPS fails, which applies to the HSTS checks.

## email — Email Security Scanner (Tool 1, v1.3.0)

**Which DMARC record applies** (v1.3.0) is found by RFC 9989's DNS tree walk (§4.10). A subdomain inherits its parent's `sp=` (or `np=`) policy. An inherited policy is stated under both RFCs and held at `suspected`, because RFC 7489 receivers choose with a Public Suffix List we do not consult (D49). Several DMARC records at one name are all discarded, as both RFCs require.

**Live basis (2026-10-08):** email live run 2, 45 domains, all 146 non-informational findings hand-verified, with checkdmarc as the external cross-check ([verification](../corpus/crosscheck/email-live/2026-10-08-run2/verification.md)). **A check that made a false claim on a live target is BROKEN until it is fixed.** DMARC claims are judged against RFC 9989, which obsoletes RFC 7489 (ledger D44).

17 checks, all passive DNS. Nothing is sent to the domain's mail servers. 9 run in Light, and 8 more in Standard.

**`confirmed` means one thing here.** A finding reaches `confirmed` when the same records, re-queried through a **second public resolver**, give the same answer (validation passed). That rules out a glitch at one resolver. **It is not independent corroboration of the record.** Both resolvers read the same authoritative servers, and both validate DNSSEC, so they fail together on a broken zone (ledger D36).

**Every email check is UNDETERMINED until the live re-verification closes (ledger D35).** The earlier live sweep was not archived, and the corpus cannot make the second-resolver step fail.

## tls — SSL/TLS Analyzer (Tool 2, v1.2.0)

19 checks, all passive under ADR-023 (ordinary TLS handshakes). 8 run in Light, and 11 more in Standard.

**No tls check has a validation step, so none reaches `confirmed`.** The ceiling is `highly_likely`. It is reached only when two independent readings agree: our own reading of the certificate, and the trust path (OpenSSL's verification). Single-reading checks stop at `suspected`.

Four Standard checks (`tls-vuln-rc4`, `-sweet32`, `-freak`, `-logjam`) are probed by **testssl.sh**, run as a separate process. Our own engine's OpenSSL cannot offer those ciphers at all.

## domscout — DNS & Subdomain Enumeration (Tool 3, v1.0.0)

6 checks, all Light, all passive. Every lookup goes to a public service (CT logs, public resolvers, RDAP), never to the target, apart from at most one ordinary HTTPS page load per takeover candidate.
